# Day 4 "Interrupts"

12:01 PM

A bunch of stuff has happened up until writing this entry.

To summarize, I had lost my ID card, my water bottle, and my sanity during my very first midterms. Although the pressure wasn't _really_ that high, I'd say that for a freshman, this is indeed new experience that I'm dealing with. One of the rubber feets of my laptop has also broken down recently, so as I'm typing this entry out for posting, no wonder how the title of it is becoming real relatable, real fast.

Well not everything is bad, however - I do have some good news to share with you. Turns out, dropping the PHY111 course was a worthwhile decision, as now I've been selected for a premier bootcamp on embedded systems and firmware engineering. They call it a "course" even though the official flyer had announced it as a bootcamp. Regardless, the first class had gone pretty smooth, which was, _before_ my midterms - it was an introductory class with all the basic elements of embedded jam-packed into a two-hour-long session. We had discussions about basic electronics and PCB design, number systems (although this is the type of stuff that most of us have covered during our higher-secondaries), and some bare-metal C which was displayed on a large projector screen. Thankfully this was a theater class that they had managed to book, otherwise it wouldn't have been as immersive.

Following the class _and_ the finishing of my mids, I've decided that I'll invest some time into embedded for good. Lately, I've been experimenting with some Rust-based HALs (or "Hardware Abstraction Layers") for the STM32 BlackPill I've bought for the class - namely, the [stm32f4xx-hal](https://crates.io/crates/stm32f4xx-hal) on crates.io - and for now its _slightly_ complicated for me. I mean, I do get how Rust works, and whatnot, but like, I'm pretty new to bare-metal MCUs. Even the AVR board I had borrowed from [Mongol-Tori](https://bracu-mongoltori.com) from a respected senior, turned out to have pretty serious HALs on crates.io. I had decided to do both C++ and Rust versions of the basic "blink" (or the "Hello World" of microcontrollers, as they say) and, it got me in, surprisingly. Oh, not to mention, interview was pretty fun as well!

Anyways, speaking more about the AVR board, I was given an Arduino Uno R3 (shoutout to Nameera, you're awesome) and the first HAL I had tried out in Rust was this one: [avr-hal](https://github.com/Rahix/avr-hal)

I wish I could attach the source that I had submitted for my aptitude test, but, oh well, I nuked it :<.

Speaking more about university, things have been going pretty chill. Even though I was kind of anxious about my differential calculus exam (because who does differential calculus right ;-; peak procrastination), turns out every math the question had was somehow similar to those from the practice sheets given during general and tutorial classes. Not to mention, I got over the exam with just _one_ PDF practice sheet, from the tutorial classroom (some of our faculties use Google Classroom - pretty convenient sometimes), and if it weren't for ONE math which needs the Leibnitz Theorem to be solved, I would've been able to grab full marks.

Ironically, I had practiced that one, single problem more than any other and still managed to forget it mid-exam. The denial was astonishing for me for the next few hours after that.

Well, otherwise, things are going really good. I still haven't touched the "Programming Rust" book, although I'm not quite sure whether this, or "the book" (if you know the reference) would be more viable for learning core concepts, I still wanna go through it anyway.

Other than that, it's almost the time for me to hit the sack.
